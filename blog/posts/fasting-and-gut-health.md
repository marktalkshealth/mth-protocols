## [Problem] A gut that never gets a rest

Your gut lining is one of the fastest-renewing tissues in your body. It's rebuilt by stem cells tucked into tiny pockets in the intestinal wall. When the gut is constantly busy digesting, and also hit by stress, poor sleep and inflammation, that repair system is under pressure.

And it's not a niche problem. Around 4 in 10 adults surveyed online in a 33-country study met the criteria for a functional gut disorder.[^sperber2021] Stress makes it worse: in healthy volunteers, a stressful public speech was enough to make the gut lining measurably leakier.[^vanuytsel2014]

## [Study] What happens to your gut when you fast

### Fasting supercharges gut stem cells (in mice)

::: study mihaylova2018
The interesting part is the mechanism. During a fast the stem cells switch fuel, from sugar to fat, and that switch seems to be what makes them more powerful. When the researchers blocked the fat-burning step, the benefit disappeared.
:::

### Fasting-mimicking cycles repaired a damaged gut (in mice)

Dr Valter Longo's lab at USC wanted to know whether fasting could help an inflamed gut, the kind seen in inflammatory bowel disease.

::: study rangan2019
Two lessons stand out for me. First, it took repeated cycles, not a single fast. Second, the version where the mice ate a little worked better than water alone.
:::

### Fasting reshaped gut bacteria in people

The strongest human evidence comes from a randomised trial in Berlin with 71 people with metabolic syndrome.

::: study maifeld2021
This is the result I share most. The fast didn't just help while people were fasting. It changed their gut bacteria, and three months later the fasting group still had lower blood pressure and a lower BMI than people who did the same healthy diet without the fast.
:::

### What the research doesn't show yet

I want to be honest about the gaps. The stem-cell and gut-repair results come from mice. The human trials so far measure gut bacteria, inflammation, blood pressure and weight, not direct repair of the human gut lining. The signal is strong and consistent, but it isn't proof that fasting heals any particular gut condition.

## [Solution] How to use fasting for your gut, safely

::: note
Fasting isn't for everyone. Talk to your doctor before fasting if you're pregnant or breastfeeding, underweight, have diabetes, take blood-pressure or blood-sugar medication, have kidney problems, or have a history of disordered eating. Longer fasts should be medically supervised.
:::

### Start with a daily overnight fast

Finish dinner a little earlier and push breakfast a little later until you reach 12 to 14 hours without food, most of it while you sleep. Many people then move to 16 hours (the 16:8 pattern). Drink water, and black coffee or plain tea if you tolerate them.

### Then try a gentle cleanse day

In my protocol there are three cleanse levels, and you only move up when the one before feels easy:

| Level | What you have | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Avocado and nut day | A whole avocado at breakfast, ⅓ cup of macadamia nuts at dinner, nothing at lunch | Beginners. Nothing to cook or blend |
| 2. Green juice day | Green vegetable juices through the day, plus mineral water | When level 1 feels easy |
| 3. Mineral water day | Mineral water and electrolytes only | Experienced fasters, ideally with medical guidance |

Notice that levels 1 and 2 still include a little food. That fits what the USC team saw: in their mice, eating a little worked better for gut repair than water alone.[^rangan2019]

### Break your fast gently

Your first meal after a fast should be small and easy to digest: eggs, a little fish or chicken, cooked vegetables, a spoonful of plain yogurt or kefir. Build back to normal portions over the day.

### Fast calm, not stressed

Here's something most fasting advice misses. A fast that leaves you anxious, wired and white-knuckling it isn't the gentle rest your gut needs. In healthy volunteers, a stressful speech made the gut lining measurably leakier, and only in people whose cortisol rose.[^vanuytsel2014]

So make your fasting days quiet ones where you can: a slow walk instead of a hard workout, ten minutes of meditation, an early night. If a fast makes you feel stressed, step back to an easier level.

### Know when to stop

End a fast and eat something if you feel faint, dizzy when you stand, confused, or your heart is racing or pounding. Those aren't signs of "detox." They're signs your body needs food, fluid or salt. Mild hunger waves and a little low energy are normal; feeling unwell isn't.

### How often?

The research points toward rhythm rather than marathons. In the USC study the benefits came from repeated cycles, not a single long fast.[^rangan2019] In the Berlin trial, one supervised 5-day fast followed by a healthy diet kept paying off three months later.[^maifeld2021]

For most people that adds up to a simple pattern: a daily overnight fast as your base, an occasional gentle cleanse day, and anything longer only with medical support.

### What to eat between fasts

The days you eat matter as much as the days you don't. Build meals around protein, fill half the plate with vegetables and add a fermented food each day. In a Stanford trial, fermented foods raised gut diversity and lowered 19 inflammation markers in ten weeks.[^wastyk2021] I break it down in [Fermented foods vs fibre: what a Stanford study found](/blog/fermented-foods-vs-fibre-stanford-study/).

## [Bottom] The bottom line

In mice, fasting boosts the stem cells that rebuild the gut lining and, done in cycles, helped repair a damaged gut.[^mihaylova2018][^rangan2019] In people, a supervised 5-day fast reshaped gut bacteria and kept blood pressure and weight lower three months later.[^maifeld2021] Start with a daily overnight fast, try a gentle cleanse day when you're ready, and check with your doctor before going longer.
